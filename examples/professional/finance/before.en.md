# Quarterly profit and operating cash flow review

This fictional manufacturer reports on an unchanged scope for the standalone April 1 through June 30 quarters of 2025 and 2026, not year-to-date periods. All amounts are exact KRW million. Net income increased by 200, or 20% of the Q2 2025 base of 1,000, while operating cash flow fell by 300 but remained positive in both quarters. Net income is a supplied bottom-line input rather than a reconstructed income statement.

## Reconciliation

For the balance rows, the table gives each quarter's opening and closing balances followed by the cash flow adjustment.

| Item | Q2 2025 | Q2 2026 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Net income | 1,000 | 1,200 |
| Depreciation expense included in net income, added back | +200 | +240 |
| Trade receivables | 800 to 900, adjustment -100 | 900 to 1,300, adjustment -400 |
| Inventory | 1,000 to 1,250, adjustment -250 | 1,200 to 1,800, adjustment -600 |
| Trade payables | 500 to 550, adjustment +50 | 600 to 760, adjustment +160 |
| Other adjustments | 0 | 0 |
| Operating cash flow | 900 | 600 |

Other adjustments total zero by design, and no acquisitions, currency translation, write-offs, reclassifications, or other noncash movements affect these balance changes. Depreciation is a noncash add-back, not a cash receipt. Accordingly, the reconciliations are `1,000 + 200 - 100 - 250 + 50 = 900` and `1,200 + 240 - 400 - 600 + 160 = 600`.

The combined working-capital adjustment moved from -300 to -840. Its additional -540 outweighed the +200 net income change and +40 depreciation adjustment change. The operating cash flow change bridge is `+200 + 40 - 300 - 350 + 110 = -300`, with the last three components reflecting the larger receivable increase, larger inventory increase, and larger payable increase, respectively.

## Interpretation limits

Cash balances, investing and financing flows, collection terms, receivable aging, inventory plans, and profit-growth causes are not supplied. Unknown is not zero. The reconciliation does not establish overdue customers, unsold inventory, or financial distress. Operating cash flow alone cannot establish a cash-balance change or support a complete liquidity assessment.
