# Warehouse automation investment review

*Fictional scenario. Prepared for the operations director and investment committee. All figures are pre-investment assumptions, not measured equipment performance. Money is in constant KRW million unless stated otherwise.*

## Decision

Management recommends authorizing only the 75 pilot tranche, because throughput and spend cancellation remain unvalidated. Neither tranche is approved or completed.

The pilot commits 165 less initially than immediate deployment. Staging, however, adds 25 in capital and delays modeled payback. Immediate deployment has better assumed economics but commits more capital before cash realization is proven.

## Operating assumptions

Four comparable zones each process 1,200 eligible cases daily for 300 operating days annually. Demand is fixed and the model assumes no sales uplift.

Of eight scheduled hours daily, 75%, or six hours, are occupied processing time. A two-worker team processes 200 cases per occupied team-hour manually. Automation targets 300 cases per occupied team-hour, which is unvalidated, with the same staffing and case mix.

At 300 cases per team-hour, processing would take four team-hours instead of six. This releases four worker-hours daily per zone, or 1,200 annually per zone and 4,800 sitewide. Scheduled time, occupied team-hours and worker-hours are different measures.

## Cash savings

Cash realization assumes the cancellation of 600 agency hours per zone annually at KRW 30,000 per invoiced hour. This yields 18 per zone. Sitewide, it cancels only 2,400 hours, not all released hours.

Permanent payroll stays unchanged. The other released hours support redeployment without cash savings. Spend cancellation remains unvalidated.

| Annual figure | Pilot (1 zone) | Sitewide (4 zones) |
|---|---|---|
| Gross cash savings | 18 | 72 |
| Incremental maintenance, software and energy cost | 4 | 12 |
| Net savings | 14 | 60 |

## Capital and payback

Immediate four-zone deployment costs 240 upfront. A one-zone pilot lasts one operating year and costs 75 upfront. Conditional expansion reuses pilot equipment but costs another 190, for a total of 265.

The model makes these assumptions:
- Spending and go-live coincide.
- There is no ramp, savings are constant, and cash accrues uniformly.
- Expansion occurs immediately after year one.
- The model excludes tax, depreciation, financing, discounting, working capital and salvage.

| Option | Upfront capital | Total capital | Simple payback |
|---|---|---|---|
| Immediate four-zone deployment | 240 | 240 | 240 / 60 = 4.00 years |
| Pilot only, no expansion | 75 | 75 | 75 / 14, about 5.36 years |
| Pilot, then expansion | 75 | 265 | 1 + (75 − 14 + 190) / 60, about 5.18 years from initial outlay |

Year one earns only 14 in the staged case.

## Conditions for expansion

Expansion would require all of the following:
- target throughput on a comparable case mix;
- a case-error rate of 0.5% or less;
- no unresolved critical safety findings;
- documented cancellation of 600 agency hours during the pilot year.

Finance and operations must also substantiate the economics of the remaining zones before a separate expansion approval.
