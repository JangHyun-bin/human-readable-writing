# Quarterly profit and operating cash flow review

This review covers a fictional manufacturer on an unchanged scope. It compares the standalone quarters of April 1 through June 30, 2025 and 2026, not year-to-date periods. All amounts are exact KRW million.

Net income rose by 200, which is 20% of the Q2 2025 base of 1,000. Operating cash flow fell by 300 but stayed positive in both quarters. Net income is a supplied bottom-line input, not a reconstructed income statement.

## Reconciliation

For the balance rows, the table gives each quarter's opening and closing balances, followed by the cash flow adjustment.

| Item | Q2 2025 | Q2 2026 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Net income | 1,000 | 1,200 |
| Depreciation expense included in net income, added back | +200 | +240 |
| Trade receivables | 800 to 900, adjustment -100 | 900 to 1,300, adjustment -400 |
| Inventory | 1,000 to 1,250, adjustment -250 | 1,200 to 1,800, adjustment -600 |
| Trade payables | 500 to 550, adjustment +50 | 600 to 760, adjustment +160 |
| Other adjustments | 0 | 0 |
| Operating cash flow | 900 | 600 |

Other adjustments total zero by design. No acquisitions, currency translation, write-offs, reclassifications, or other noncash movements affect these balance changes. Depreciation is a noncash add-back, not a cash receipt. The reconciliations are therefore:

- Q2 2025: `1,000 + 200 - 100 - 250 + 50 = 900`
- Q2 2026: `1,200 + 240 - 400 - 600 + 160 = 600`

The combined working-capital adjustment moved from -300 to -840. That additional -540 outweighed the +200 change in net income and the +40 change in the depreciation add-back.

The bridge for the change in operating cash flow is `+200 + 40 - 300 - 350 + 110 = -300`. The first two terms are the changes in net income and the depreciation add-back. The last three reflect the larger receivable increase (-300), the larger inventory increase (-350), and the larger payable increase (+110), respectively.

## Interpretation limits

The source does not supply cash balances, investing and financing flows, collection terms, receivable aging, inventory plans, or the causes of profit growth. Unknown is not zero.

The reconciliation does not establish overdue customers, unsold inventory, or financial distress. Operating cash flow alone cannot establish a change in the cash balance or support a complete liquidity assessment.
