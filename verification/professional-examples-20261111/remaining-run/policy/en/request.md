# Evidence request on New York congestion pricing

To the transport-policy reviewer

## Reported results and missing definitions

Page 13 of MTA's December 17, 2025 board presentation, *2025 in Data*, reports 11% fewer vehicle entries into the central business district, or CBD, and 71,000 fewer per day. These are entry counts, not unique cars or vehicle-miles, and the rounded figures do not establish an exact baseline count. Observation and comparison periods and baseline construction are unspecified on the slide.

MTA also reports car speeds within the CBD up 4.3%, which does not mean travel time fell 4.3%. Absolute speeds, weighting, hours covered, measurement provider, baseline, and observation and comparison periods are unspecified.

MTA reports PM2.5 down 22% in the first six months, crediting Cornell University, 2025. The slide places this claim under a Congestion Relief Zone heading without specifying exact study dates, the concentration statistic, the percentage denominator, or detailed geographic coverage. The concentration claim does not establish a 22% reduction in emissions, all pollutants, or individual exposure, and cannot be generalized citywide. The presentation title does not establish a common full-year comparison for these metrics.

## Evidence requested

I treat the implementing agency's summary as a record of attributed claims, not independent causal proof of the policy's effects. For independent review, I request the traffic series and calculation methods, the missing periods and baselines, and the underlying air-quality study's geography, statistic, counterfactual method, and uncertainty. This is my request for evidence, not MTA's recommendation or a recommendation to repeal or expand congestion pricing.
